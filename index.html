Real-time ergonomic risk assessment in VR (RULA)

Master's research internship at the G-SCOP laboratory, supervised by Dr. Mathieu Delangle. Traditional ergonomic assessment can't monitor a worker continuously — this system watches posture in real time inside an immersive VR workstation and adapts it the moment a risky posture is detected, designed as a support tool for ergonomists and workstation designers rather than a replacement for their expertise.

Python · scikit-learn · Unreal Engine 5 · OptiTrack · TCP sockets

  • Built a dataset of 19,332 labeled samples from OptiTrack and Meta Quest motion capture, computing six joint angles per side in Unreal Engine and labeling each sample automatically with a rule-based function adapted from the RULA method.
  • Trained and compared Random Forest and SVM classifiers to predict risk level (low, medium, high); the Random Forest reached 96.5% test accuracy with stronger recall on high-risk postures — the metric that matters when a missed detection means an unaddressed injury risk — and was deployed live through a TCP server.
  • Visualized predicted risk in real time through skeleton coloring and an overhead risk sphere, then closed the loop as a proof of concept: the workstation's table height rises automatically the moment a high-risk left-shoulder posture is detected.
